Where do I get a link for my own photo? A photo on your phone or laptop has no URL, so it has to go somewhere public first. Whatever you use, the link must point straight at the image file and start with https:// — paste it into a private/incognito window, and if you see the bare picture and nothing else, it will work here.

  • Wikipedia / Wikimedia Commons — open the picture, right-click it and choose Copy image address. You get an upload.wikimedia.org/… link, which is free, permanent and the most reliable option.
  • Google Drive — supported natively, just paste the share link. Upload the photo, set Share → General access → Anyone with the link, copy the link and drop it into a square exactly as Google gives it to you. This page recognises it and rewrites it into a direct image link for you. The one thing you have to get right is the sharing setting: on "Restricted" the recipient sees a login screen instead of your photo.
  • Your own website, or any image host — Imgur (use the i.imgur.com/… address), GitHub's Raw button, S3, Cloudinary. Or drag a folder of photos onto Netlify Drop for instant public URLs, no account needed.
  • No hosting at all — a picture can travel inside the link as a data: URI. Nothing gets uploaded anywhere, nothing can expire or be taken down, and nobody but the recipient ever sees it. The cost is length: each picture adds thousands of characters to the finished link, and very long links get mangled by some chat apps. Keep the images small, and don't do it for all nine squares.
    How to turn a photo into a data: URI
    1. Shrink it first. A square is shown at roughly 200×200 pixels, so scale the photo to about 400×400 and save it as WebP or JPEG at moderate quality. A 3 MB phone photo would add roughly 4 million characters to the link; a 20 KB thumbnail adds about 27 000.
    2. Convert it on your own machine. On macOS or Linux:
      base64 -w0 photo.webp
      On Windows (PowerShell):
      [Convert]::ToBase64String((Get-Content photo.webp -AsByteStream))
    3. Put the prefix in front of what you got: data:image/webp;base64, — or data:image/jpeg;base64, for a JPEG, data:image/png;base64, for a PNG. The prefix has to match the actual file type.
    4. Paste the whole string (prefix + the long block of letters, no spaces or line breaks) into the square's box above. This page URL-encodes it for you.

    Online base64 converters work too, but most of them upload your photo to their server first — which defeats the point. Use one only if it states that it converts in your browser, or stick to the commands above.

Google Photos and iCloud share links are album pages, not files, and won't work. Squares are shown at roughly 200×200 pixels and cropped to a square, so small, roughly square pictures look best. Shrinking a photo down before turning it into a data: URI is covered in the documentation.
